# Listing Optimization Playbook

> A step-by-step workflow for turning any Amazon product into a fully optimized listing — from raw inputs to title, bullets, images, and A+ content.

---

## What This Is

Most Amazon listings **describe** the product. This playbook helps you **sell** it.

The difference: selling requires knowing the product deeply (not just what it is, but why it matters), knowing the buyer deeply (not just who they are, but what stops them from clicking "Add to Cart"), and then expressing the right things in the right places across every inch of the listing.

This playbook is a 7-phase pipeline. Each phase builds on the previous one. At each phase, the AM reviews and validates before moving forward.

---

## The Pipeline at a Glance

```
INPUTS → RESEARCH → PRODUCT KNOWLEDGE → BUYER PERSONAS → ATTRIBUTES → KEYWORDS → COPY + DESIGN
```

| Phase | What It Produces | AM Checkpoint? |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Inputs** | Raw materials (ASIN, website, competitor list, brand context) | - |
| **1. Research** | Evidence corpus (reviews, competitors, cultural context, search data) | Yes — "Did we miss anything?" |
| **2. Product Knowledge Base** | Deep understanding of what this product truly is | Yes — "Is this accurate?" |
| **3. Buyer Personas** | Who buys this and what drives their decision | Yes — "Do we recognize these buyers?" |
| **4. Attributes & Features** | Prioritized list of everything that matters, mapped to buyers | Yes — "Is the priority right?" |
| **5. Keywords** | Categorized, prioritized keyword strategy | - |
| **6. Listing Copy** | Multiple title and bullet point options | Yes — "Which options do we pick?" |
| **7. Design Brief** | Image strategy + A+ content strategy with creative directions | Yes — "Which directions do we pursue?" |

## Inputs: What We Start With

We can work with any combination of these. More inputs = richer output.

| Input | What It Gives Us |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Existing Amazon listing** (ASIN) | Current title, bullets, images, reviews, Q&A, price, rating, category |
| **D2C website** | Brand story, richer product descriptions, visual identity, testimonials, FAQ |
| **Social media** (Instagram, TikTok) | Brand voice, content that resonates, user-generated content, lifestyle context |

**Minimum viable input:** Just an ASIN. But the more context we have, the better the output.

## Phase 1: Research & Discovery

### What we're doing
Digging deep into the product from every angle — not just reading the listing, but understanding the cultural context, mining reviews (ours and competitors'), studying what people actually search for, and finding evidence from Reddit, blogs, and food media.

### Seven research domains

**1. Product deep-dive** — What is this product at its most fundamental level? What are the ingredients/materials? What's the manufacturing process? Does the process matter to buyers?

**2. Cultural & historical context** — Does this product have regional significance? Is there a heritage story? Is there a trend it connects to (e.g., clean eating, artisan food)? What does Reddit say about this category?

**3. Competitive landscape** — Who competes directly? Who competes indirectly? What does each competitor do well and poorly? Where is the white space?

**4. Review mining** — What do 5-star reviewers specifically praise (exact language matters)? What do 1-3 star reviewers complain about? What comparisons do they make? What do competitor reviews reveal?

**5. Search behavior** — What do people actually type? What are the auto-complete suggestions? What keywords do top competitors rank for?

**6. Category norms** — What does a typical listing look like? What image styles dominate? What price range is normal? What bullet themes are common?

**7. Brand ecosystem** — What else does this brand sell? Is there cross-sell potential? What's the brand store like?

### Maurice's Example: Research Findings

```

• Mustard-based BBQ sauce (South Carolina regional style)
• 2-pack, 36oz total (2 × 18oz), $22-24 ($0.61/oz)
• 4.7 stars, 989 reviews, Amazon's Choice
• All-natural, no HFCS, no preservatives
• From Maurice's Piggie Park restaurants (est. 1953), same recipe for 70+ years

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
• SC mustard BBQ is one of four regional BBQ styles in the US
  (KC tomato, TX vinegar-pepper, NC vinegar, SC mustard)
• Most Americans default to tomato-based = education opportunity

OUR REVIEWS SAY:
• Love: "Best mustard BBQ", "just like the restaurant", "perfect on pulled pork"
• Hate: "Too tangy for some", "expected more heat", "price seems high"

SEARCH DATA:

```

## Phase 2: Product Knowledge Base

### What we're doing
Synthesizing all that research into a structured understanding of what this product **truly is** — not just its physical attributes, but its essence, its cultural place, and its hierarchy of value propositions.

### Product Essence: Five Layers

Every product has five layers of identity:

| Layer | Question | Maurice's Answer |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| **Category** | What is it? | Mustard-based BBQ sauce |
| **Functional** | What does it do? | Flavors and enhances grilled/smoked meats |
| **Emotional** | What does it mean? | A taste of South Carolina BBQ heritage |
| **Cultural** | What cultural context? | SC mustard BBQ is a distinct American tradition, the most underrated regional style |
| **Origin** | Where does it come from? | Maurice's Piggie Park, SC, est. 1953 — same recipe for 70+ years |

### Uniqueness Analysis: Where Does the Uniqueness Live?

Uniqueness can live in different places. For each, we ask: how rare is it, can competitors copy it, and does the buyer actually care?

| Dimension | Maurice's Uniqueness | Rare? | Defensible? |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|-------------|
| **Recipe** | Mustard-based (only ~5% of BBQ sauces) | High | Medium — recipe can be copied, but heritage can't |
| **Process** | Same recipe, same facility as the restaurants | Medium | High — can't fake this |
| **Origin** | From a legendary 70+ year SC restaurant chain | High | High — heritage can't be manufactured |
| **Experience** | Tangy, bold, completely different from tomato BBQ | High | Low — taste can be approximated |
| **Category** | Defining product in an underrepresented sub-category | High | High — first-mover in the niche |

### Value Proposition Hierarchy

Not all value props are equal. They stack in order of importance:

```
CORE (the ONE thing):
"Authentic South Carolina mustard BBQ sauce from a legendary 70-year restaurant chain"

PRIMARY (directly supports the core):
1. Restaurant heritage = proven, trusted recipe
2. Mustard-based = unique flavor you can't find elsewhere
3. All-natural ingredients, no HFCS

SECONDARY (adds value but isn't the reason to buy):
1. 2-pack format = better value for regular users
2. Versatile — pork, chicken, ribs, dipping, marinades
3. Amazon's Choice + 989 reviews at 4.7★

TERTIARY (nice-to-have for specific segments):
1. Makes a great gift for BBQ lovers
2. Prime shipping
3. Multi-flavor variety available
```

**Does the buyer need education?** Yes — many buyers don't know mustard BBQ exists as a style. The listing must teach: *"This is not a regular BBQ sauce. It's a completely different tradition."*

**AM checkpoint:** "Is the essence right? Is the value prop hierarchy the right priority? Does the client agree with this positioning?"

## Phase 3: Buyer Personas

### What we're doing
Building evidence-based profiles of who actually buys this product — not demographics on paper, but what drives their decision, what stops them, what they search for, and what they need to see (but didn't ask for) to convert.

### Where Personas Come From

| Source | What It Reveals |
|--------|----------------|
| Our reviews | Who actually buys, what they value, how they describe the product |
| Competitor reviews | Who buys alternatives, what they wish was different |
| Search terms | How different segments think about the category |
| Reddit/forums | Unfiltered discussions about purchase decisions |
| Q&A section | Specific pre-purchase concerns |

## Phase 4: Product Attributes & Features

### What we're doing
Creating a **master list** of every product attribute that could matter to any buyer. Each attribute is tagged by type, priority, which personas care about it, and where it should show up on the listing.

### Attribute Types

| Type | What it means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Feature** | A factual characteristic | "Mustard-based recipe" |
| **Uniqueness** | Differentiates from competitors | "70+ year restaurant heritage" |
| **Problem Solver** | Addresses a customer pain | "No HFCS — clean label for the ingredient-conscious" |
| **Trust Signal** | Builds credibility | "989 reviews at 4.7 stars" |
| **Experience** | Describes the outcome of using it | "Tangy, bold flavor unlike any store-bought sauce" |
| **Social Proof** | External validation | "Amazon's Choice badge" |
| **Convenience** | Makes buying/using easier | "2-pack so you don't run out mid-season" |
| **Value** | Addresses price-to-worth | "Restaurant-quality sauce at grocery prices" |

### Priority Levels

| Priority | Meaning | Where It Must Appear |
|----------|---------|---------------------|
| **P0** | If the buyer doesn't see this, they won't buy | Title + first 2 bullets + images |
| **P1** | Strengthens the decision, important for comparisons | Bullets + images or A+ |
| **P2** | Adds value for specific segments | A+ or backend keywords |
| **P3** | Supporting facts for indexing or edge cases | Backend keywords only |

## Phase 5: Keyword Strategy

### What we're doing
Building a categorized, prioritized keyword list. Every keyword gets a type, a priority, and a placement plan (where it should appear in the listing).

### Keyword Types

| Type | What it is | Example | Volume |
|------|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Master** | Core category term | "bbq sauce" | Highest |
| **Sub-category** | More specific category | "mustard bbq sauce", "carolina bbq sauce" | Medium |
| **Long-tail** | Multi-word specific phrase | "south carolina mustard bbq sauce for pulled pork" | Lower, higher intent |
| **Branded** | Includes our brand | "maurice's bbq sauce", "piggie park" | Low, highest intent |
| **Competitor branded** | Includes competitor brand | "sweet baby ray's alternative" | Low |
| **Problem/use-case** | Describes a need | "sauce for pulled pork", "best grilling sauce" | Medium |
| **Question** | Framed as a question | "what is mustard bbq sauce" | Low |
| **Seasonal** | Time-bound | "bbq sauce for memorial day" | Spiky |
| **Cross-sell** | Adjacent product | "bbq gift basket", "southern food box" | Low |

### Where Keywords Go

```
TITLE (150 chars, front-load the important ones):
├── Position 1: Brand name → "Maurice's"
├── Position 2: Product name → "Southern Gold BBQ Sauce"
├── Position 3: Category keyword → "Carolina Mustard Barbecue Sauce"
├── Position 4: Differentiator → "Original Restaurant Recipe"
└── Position 5: Format → "18oz (Pack of 2)"

BULLETS (indexed, high weight):
├── Bullet 1: P0 feature keyword + benefit
├── Bullet 2: P0 uniqueness keyword + benefit
├── Bullet 3: P1 problem-solving keyword + benefit
├── Bullet 4: P1 use-case keyword + benefit
└── Bullet 5: P1 value keyword + benefit

BACKEND KEYWORDS (250 bytes, catch everything else):
└── P2/P3 keywords, misspellings, synonyms, regional terms
    "barbeque bar-b-que dipping pulled pork smoker ribs grilling
     marinade condiment tangy southern gold SC regional gourmet
     artisan gift cookout tailgate summer picnic gluten free"

A+ CONTENT (alt text only):
└── Question keywords, comparison keywords, use-case keywords
```

## Phase 6: Listing Copy — Title & Bullets

### What we're doing
Generating **multiple options** for the title and bullet points. The AM picks the best combination.

### Amazon Title Rules

- Max 200 characters (150 recommended for mobile readability)
- Brand name first (Amazon policy)
- First ~80 characters show on mobile — front-load what matters
- No ALL CAPS, no promotional language ("Best Seller"), no special characters
- Every word is indexed for Amazon search

### Title Options for Maurice's

**Option A — Keyword-Optimized:**
> Maurice's Southern Gold BBQ Sauce – Carolina Mustard Barbecue Sauce, All Natural, No HFCS – Authentic SC Restaurant Recipe – 18oz (Pack of 2)

Strategy: Front-loads "BBQ Sauce" and "Carolina Mustard" for maximum search indexing.

## Phase 7: Design Brief — Images & A+ Content

### What we're doing
Creating detailed briefs for every product image slot and every A+ content module — with multiple creative directions per slot, and a strategy for **threading** key messages across all visual elements.

### The Key Concept: Trust Threading

Trust isn't built in one image. It's **woven** across the entire listing experience. If "authenticity" is important, the buyer should feel it in the main image, see it in the lifestyle shot, read it in the infographic, and absorb it in the A+ hero banner — not just in a single "authenticity slide."

```
EXAMPLE — Threading "This is authentic/real" across the listing:

Main Image:     Professional bottle shot (premium feel signals quality)
Image 3:        Sauce on real food at a real BBQ (not stock photography)
Image 6:        Restaurant storefront photo + "Same Recipe Since 1953"
A+ Module 1:    Heritage story banner with historical imagery
A+ Module 4:    Comparison chart (stands up to scrutiny = nothing to hide)
A+ Module 5:    Lifestyle — real people, real backyard BBQ
Bullet 2:       "THE REAL THING, NOT A KNOCKOFF"
```

The buyer never sees a single "authenticity slide." They **absorb** authenticity across 7 touchpoints.

### Three Trust Threads for Maurice's

| Trust Signal | Touchpoints |
|-------------|-------------|
| **"This is authentic"** | Main image (premium label), Image 3 (real food), Image 6 (restaurant photo), A+ Module 1 (heritage story), A+ Module 4 (comparison chart), Bullet 2 |
| **"This is safe to buy"** | Image 5 (ingredients), Bullet 3 (no HFCS), Bullet 5 (989 reviews, 4.7★), A+ Module 4 (transparency), A+ Module 6 (FAQ) |
| **"This is worth the price"** | Image 4 ("restaurant-grade" infographic), Bullet 2 (same as restaurant), A+ Module 1 (heritage = you're paying for authenticity), A+ Module 3 (all natural = quality costs more), Bullet 5 (2-pack value) |

#### Slot 1: Main Image — Stop the scroll

#### Slot 2: Close-Up — Show what's inside

| **3** | Feature Highlights | Communicate three pillars | Mustard-Based • All Natural • SC Heritage — each with icon, photo, and short text |
