# Product Understanding: Goal Crazy

## Product Profile: P0 - Goal Crazy 90-Day Undated Planner (Black)

**ASIN:** B07QNSB373

**Product:**
- A5-sized (5.875" x 8.25") 90-day undated planner with PU leather hardcover, designed as a guided goal-setting and productivity system
- What makes it unique: it's not just a blank planner. It includes a goal discovery course, reflection prompts, scripting/affirmation sections, and detachable habit card bookmarks. Positions itself as "a personal coach in book form"
- Solves the gap between wanting to achieve goals and having a structured system to actually track and execute on them daily
- Core purchase motivation: people who want to get more organized and goal-oriented but need external structure and accountability. The 90-day format makes it feel achievable rather than overwhelming (vs. a full-year planner)

**Customer:**
- Target buyer: goal-oriented professionals, entrepreneurs, students, and self-improvement enthusiasts. Skews toward people who consume productivity content (books, podcasts, courses) and want a physical tool to complement that
- The purchase is driven by a desire for structure and accountability. These buyers have likely tried generic planners before and found them insufficient because they lacked guided prompts, habit tracking, or a goal-setting framework

**Brand:**
- Registered brand with real identity. Founded by Jason VanDevere (Akron, OH), who built the planner methodology after interviewing successful individuals for 7+ months. Has a founder story, press coverage (Fast Company, Money.com), and an ecosystem beyond just the product
- Brand maturity: Amazon-native, launched July 2019 (~7 years old). Has expanded into DTC (goalcrazy.com, migrated from Shopify to GoHighLevel), coaching, courses, a book ("Goal Crazy: How To Start And Grow A Dream Driven Business"), and a podcast
- Notable signals: professional website exists but recently migrated to a coaching/funnel platform (GoHighLevel), suggesting a pivot toward coaching revenue. Has Facebook, Twitter/X, Pinterest, LinkedIn. No Instagram or TikTok presence, which is a significant gap for the planner niche where "plan with me" content drives discovery and sales
- Brand vibe: motivational and action-oriented with a slightly masculine lean. Navy/teal and gold palette. "Be CRAZY About Your Goals | Take CRAZY Action | Live a CRAZY Life." Professional but not aesthetically refined. Competes on methodology and substance rather than visual appeal, which differentiates it from the pastel/aesthetic planner brands but also limits its appeal to the large stationery/planner community on social media

**Listing Quality and Conversion Drivers:**

- **Rating:** 4.4 from 1,063 reviews. Distribution is healthy: 72% five-star, 12% four-star, 8% three-star, 5% two-star, 3% one-star. No red flags in the distribution
- **Title:** 199 characters. Keyword-dense and covers the major search terms well (undated planner, 90 day, guided journal, productivity organizer, daily & weekly, goal setting, habit tracker, A5, leather hardcover). However, the brand name "GOAL CRAZY" is missing from the title. For a brand that has built real identity and a founder story, this is a missed opportunity for brand recognition and branded search capture. The title also uses a long dash in the first word, which could be replaced with a comma or colon for cleaner formatting
- **A+ Content:** Premium A+ is present with 7 image modules, but it is entirely image-based with zero text (0 word count). Every module is a standalone image with no accompanying copy. This means the A+ content is not indexed for search and misses the opportunity to reinforce key selling points (the coaching methodology, the habit cards, the paper quality) in text that Amazon can crawl. For a product whose main differentiator is its methodology, the A+ should explain that methodology, not just show lifestyle images
- **Images:** 7 images. The main image shows the planner with its belly band and the habit card bookmarks. Clean white background, product is clear. However, the main image does not show the interior, which is this product's biggest differentiator. A shopper scrolling search results sees a generic black planner. They cannot tell this is a guided coaching system without clicking in. Showing open pages with the habit tracker or goal-setting framework on the main image (or via a badge) would significantly improve CTR
- **Video:** 6 videos including seller-produced, influencer, and customer videos. Multiple are over 30 seconds. Strong video presence, this is a clear advantage over competitors

**Rating Trajectory:** Declining, gradually

- Started at 5.0 (Jul 2019), dropped to 4.7 by late 2019 as reviews accumulated

## Insights

- P0 (Goal Crazy 90-Day Planner) has a strong product with real differentiation (coaching methodology, habit cards, founder story) but is losing ground steadily. Sales rank in the Planners subcategory has roughly doubled (worsened) from 2024 to 2026. The product and reviews are solid, so the decline is likely driven by competitive pressure, reduced ad spend, or failure to evolve the listing and marketing
- P0's A+ Content is Premium but entirely image-based with zero text. This wastes the SEO value of A+ (Amazon indexes A+ text) and fails to communicate the product's core differentiator (the coaching methodology) to shoppers who scroll past the bullets. Competitors in this space use text-heavy A+ to explain their frameworks
- P0's main image shows a closed black planner, which is visually indistinguishable from dozens of competitors on the search results page. The product's biggest selling point (guided goal-setting system with habit trackers, reflection prompts, scripting sections) is invisible until you click into the listing
- The brand has no Instagram or TikTok presence. The planner community on these platforms is massive ("plan with me" content, stationery hauls, planner reviews), and this is a significant missed channel for organic discovery and social proof

## Things to Investigate Further

- The sustained sales rank decline needs to be cross-referenced with ad spend trends (Step 4). If ad spend has been reduced or campaigns have become less efficient, that could explain the rank decline without any product or listing issue
- Check SQP data (Step 3) to see if impression share on core keywords has dropped over time, which would confirm whether the decline is a visibility problem
- Check whether competitors (Panda Planner, 90X, This Is My Era) have gained share on the same keywords Goal Crazy targets
